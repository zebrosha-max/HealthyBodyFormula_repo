Sleep Biochemistry:
Deep Recovery

Sleep is the time of your biological "cleanup." While you sleep, the brain's glymphatic system works, clearing toxic proteins, and the endocrine system synthesizes growth hormone and restores receptor sensitivity.

This protocol will help you synchronize your nutrition and routine with daylight to make sleep the most productive tool for health and longevity.

Part 1: Hormonal Balance

Melatonin vs Cortisol

Ideally, cortisol is high in the morning (wakes us up), and melatonin — at night (puts us to sleep). Late eating (especially sweets) causes an insulin spike that suppresses melatonin and growth hormone. The result: you sleep 8 hours but wake up exhausted.

Part 2: Adjustment Protocol

1. "Sleepy" Dinner

Essence: Dinner 3-4 hours before bed. It should be light but contain tryptophan — an amino acid precursor to melatonin.

Best foods: Turkey, eggs, cherries (contain natural melatonin), walnuts, pumpkin seeds.

Tip: A small amount of complex carbohydrates at dinner (e.g., half a cup of rice or quinoa) helps tryptophan cross the blood-brain barrier more easily and convert to serotonin, then melatonin.

2. Light Regime

Essence: Block blue spectrum light 2 hours before bed.

Action: Blue light from smartphones signals "noon" to the brain, blocking melatonin for 3-4 hours. Use "night mode" on devices or special blue light blocking glasses (Blue blockers).

3. Nutraceutical Support

Magnesium Glycinate: 400 mg 1 hour before bed. Glycine in this magnesium form is an inhibitory neurotransmitter that "turns off" racing thoughts.

Herbal tea: Lemon balm, chamomile, or passionflower — gentle support for GABA receptors.

Part 3: Troubleshooting

1. Waking at 3-4 AM

Cause 1: Blood sugar drop (hypoglycemia). The brain releases cortisol to raise sugar — and you wake up feeling anxious.

Cause 2: Peak liver detox activity. If the liver is overloaded (late dinner, alcohol), it may "wake" you.

Solution: Eliminate evening alcohol and add a bit more healthy fats to dinner.
