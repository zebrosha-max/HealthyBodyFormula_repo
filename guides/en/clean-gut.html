GI Health

Clean Gut:
Foundation of Immunity

28 days Science-based

The gut is not just a tube for digesting food. It's the largest immune organ, containing 70-80% of immune-competent cells and trillions of bacteria that control your mood, metabolism, and even genetics.

This protocol aims to restore the intestinal barrier and eliminate systemic inflammation, which often manifests as fatigue, brain fog, and skin problems.

Part 1: Scientific Basis

Increased Permeability Syndrome

Normally, intestinal walls are a "smart filter." When inflamed, tight junctions between cells weaken, allowing toxins, undigested proteins, and lipopolysaccharides (LPS) to enter the bloodstream. This triggers an immune response that we experience as chronic fatigue or acne.

Part 2: Protocol Implementation

The protocol is divided into logical stages of mucosal and microbiome recovery.

Phase 1: Elimination (Days 1-14)

Goal: Remove inflammation "triggers" to reduce the load on the immune system.

What to eliminate: Gluten, whole milk, added sugar, and refined oils. These products often cause temporary intolerance when the mucosa is damaged.

Action: Transition to whole foods: vegetables, quality protein (fish, poultry), healthy fats.

Phase 2: Regeneration (Days 15-28)

Goal: Supply "building material" for intestinal cells (enterocytes).

Key elements:

  • Bone broth: Source of collagen and amino acids (L-glutamine) that literally "seal" damage in the walls.
  • Fiber: Colorful vegetables as prebiotics for friendly flora growth.
  • Aloe vera (juice): Has a powerful healing effect on mucous membranes.

Part 3: Practical Diet

Ally Foods

  • Fermented vegetables: Sugar-free sauerkraut (start with 1 tsp if you experience bloating).
  • Cruciferous: Broccoli, cauliflower (better heat-treated).
  • Fatty fish: Omega-3 reduces local inflammation.
  • Herbs: Oregano, garlic, and ginger have natural antiseptic properties.

Part 4: Challenges and Solutions

Gut recovery is a journey where it's important to properly respond to body signals.

1. Bloating from Fiber

Cause: The microbiota isn't ready to process large amounts of fiber yet.

Solution: Heat treatment (stewing, baking, pureeing). Introduce raw vegetables in minimal portions after 2 weeks of the protocol.

2. Die-off Reaction

Symptoms: Short-term increase in acne or weakness.

Cause: Death of pathogenic flora and release of their metabolites.

Solution: Drink more clean water, use sorbents (activated charcoal or pectin) 1.5 hours before meals for 3-5 days.

3. Social Dinners

Solution: Choose the simplest dishes possible: grilled (meat/fish) + vegetable side dish. Ask the waiter not to add sauces and dress salads with oil instead of mayonnaise.

Individual Adaptation

If you have a confirmed diagnosis of SIBO (Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth), consuming fermented products may worsen symptoms. In this case, transitioning to a Low FODMAP protocol under specialist supervision is necessary.

Safety

Contraindications: Acute peptic ulcer disease, ulcerative colitis (UC) in acute phase, Crohn's disease. This protocol does not replace medication prescribed by a gastroenterologist.

Stop signals: Acute abdominal pain, blood in stool, sudden fever.
