Anti-Fragility

Cortisol Control:
Nutrition Against Stress

Stress is a biologically expensive reaction. In survival mode, your body spends enormous amounts of resources (magnesium, B vitamins, vitamin C) to support the nervous system and synthesize stress hormones.

If these resources aren't replenished, the "exhaustion" stage sets in, which in nutritional science is called adrenal dysfunction. This guide will help you create a nutritive "safety cushion" and regain control over your state.

Part 1: The Nutrient "Theft" Mechanism

Resource Deficit

The adrenal glands are the main consumers of vitamin C and magnesium in the body. Under stress, the kidneys start excreting magnesium much faster. The result: muscle tension, insomnia, and salt cravings (an attempt to retain electrolytes).

Part 2: Support Protocol

1. "Anti-Stress" Breakfast

Why it matters: Under stress, the morning cortisol peak can be too high or, conversely, absent. We need to stabilize blood sugar from the morning.

Protocol: Breakfast within the first hour after waking. Must include: protein + fats + complex carbohydrates. For example: omelet with vegetables and a piece of whole grain bread.

Prohibition: Coffee on an empty stomach. Caffeine stimulates cortisol release when it's already peaking, leading to rapid resource depletion by lunchtime.

2. Electrolyte Replenishment

Goal: Support water-salt balance.

  • Quality salt: Himalayan or sea salt (source of 80+ microelements). Don't be afraid to salt food to taste — adrenals need sodium.
  • Magnesium baths: Epsom salt (English salt) — magnesium absorbs well through the skin, relaxing the nervous system before sleep.
3. Ally Micronutrients

Vitamin C: Rose hip, bell peppers, parsley. The adrenal glands store the highest concentration of vitamin C in the body.

B Vitamins: Organ meats, seeds, eggs. Necessary for nerve impulse transmission and energy production.

Part 3: Troubleshooting

1. Evening "Binge Eating" and Sugar Cravings

Cause: The brain is trying to quickly get glucose to "quench" high evening cortisol.

Solution: A hearty balanced dinner with sufficient fiber. If craving is intense — take 400 mg of magnesium glycinate.
